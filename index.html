Special Issue on Argument Mining in the Legal Domain

August 1, 2026 - August 1, 2027

About

The legal domain is a natural setting for applying argument models and machine learning to extract and analyse reasoning structures. Legal texts are inherently argumentative, and tools that can automatically detect, classify, and connect arguments would greatly enhance legal analytics tasks such as summarisation, classification, and decision support.

While Large Language Models (LLMs) have transformed many NLP tasks, argument mining in the legal domain remains underexplored. Legal documents pose distinctive challenges: their length, specialised terminology, and the background knowledge required to interpret them. Moreover, differences across jurisdictions, languages, and legal subfields complicate the development of generalisable solutions.

At the same time, argument mining has gained renewed significance in NLP, driven by the growing focus on reasoning and interpretability in AI. Argumentation provides a useful framework for evaluating how models reason - e.g., how they construct, justify, and assess conclusions from evidence.

This makes the present moment particularly timely for a special issue on argument mining in the legal domain. Specifically, three developments motivate this proposal: (1) the growing maturity of LLMs capable of processing complex texts, (2) the increasing demand for explainable AI in high-stakes areas such as law, and (3) the expanding availability of annotated legal corpora and open legal data.

This special issue seeks to advance these developments by bringing together contributions that explore both foundational and applied aspects of legal argument mining. Topics of interest include argumentation schemas and annotation frameworks for legal texts, new argument mining datasets and corpora, novel architectures and learning methods, and the integration of argument mining into downstream legal NLP tasks such as summarisation, classification, and decision support.

Call for Papers

Topics of Interest.

The topics for submissions include but are not limited to:

  • Development of new legal corpora and annotation frameworks for argument mining
  • Novel methods and models for detecting, classifying, and linking argumentative components in legal texts
  • Adaptation and evaluation of existing argument mining techniques in legal and cross-jurisdictional contexts
  • Use of argument mining for legal analytics, including summarisation, classification, retrieval, and decision support
  • Studies of argumentative reasoning and structure in legal discourse, case law, and judicial decision-making
  • Evaluation frameworks and benchmarks for assessing argument mining systems in the legal domain

We welcome submissions from all areas of application.

Submission Details.

The organizing committee welcomes submitting papers through the AI & Law journal's platform. Submissions must describe substantial, original, completed, and unpublished work. Wherever appropriate, concrete evaluation and analysis should be included. Refer to AI & Law's submission guidelines for more details.

Double Blind Review.

Submissions are bouble-blind. Papers must not include authors' names and affiliations. Furthermore, self-references or links (such as GitHub) that reveal the author’s identity, e.g., “We previously showed (Smith, 1991) …” must be avoided. Instead, use citations such as “Smith previously showed (Smith, 1991) …” Papers that do not conform to these requirements will be rejected without review. Papers should not refer, for further detail, to documents that are not available to the reviewers. For example, do not omit or redact important citation information to preserve anonymity. Instead, use the third person or named reference to this work, as described above (“Smith showed” rather than “we showed”). Papers may be accompanied by a resource (software and/or data) described in the paper, but these resources should also be anonymized.
